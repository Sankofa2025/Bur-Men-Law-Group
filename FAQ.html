IL Intoxicated Driving FAQ

Driving while intoxicated (DWI), operating a vehicle under the influence (OVI), or driving under the influence (DUI) describe the same offense. In IL, there are many arrests made daily for intoxicated driving. At Bur-Men Law Group, our DUI defense team works hard to help you beat a DUI charge or achieve the best possible outcome in your unique situation. Contact us at (224) 637-8872 to learn more. In the meantime, here are answers to commonly asked questions.

What is "blood alcohol content" (BAC) level?

Blood alcohol content (BAC) is a measurement of the amount of alcohol found in the blood expressed as a percentage. It is calculated in grams per 210 liters of breath, and a BAC of 0.08 means there is 0.08% alcohol by volume. Measuring BAC is a way for law enforcement to calculate the amount of alcohol someone has had and their ability to drive a motor vehicle.

What are my rights during a DUI traffic stop?

If you are pulled over due to suspicion of drunk driving, you have certain rights as a U.S. citizen:

  • The driver and any passengers have the right to remain silent (except you must show the police your driver's license, registration, and proof of insurance upon request).
  • If you are a passenger, you are generally free to leave.
  • If you are arrested or detained, you have additional rights, including Miranda warnings.

You can say you wish to remain silent and ask for a lawyer immediately. You have the right to make a local phone call. If you believe your rights were violated, try to write down everything you remember, including the police officer's agency, badge number, and patrol car number.

Can I refuse a breathalyzer test in Lake County?

You can refuse a breathalyzer test. However, refusal carries significant consequences, which may include:

  • Your license may be suspended.
  • You may still face a DUI charge based on other criteria, such as field sobriety test results, witness testimony, and the officer's observations.
  • You may be fined or face jail time.

Additionally, if you refuse, the police officer may request a warrant to take a blood sample. Blood tests are more reliable than breath tests and can be more challenging to defend against.

What are standardized field sobriety tests (FSTs)?

Standardized FSTs are tests approved by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) designed to help police determine whether a driver is intoxicated. There are three standardized FSTs:

  • The Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus Test (HGN)
  • The One-Leg Stand Test (OLS)
  • The Walk-and-Turn Test

Non-standardized tests (e.g., finger to nose, alphabet test, coin pickup) are not validated by NHTSA and are typically not admissible as evidence.

Can I refuse field sobriety tests in IL?

The ability to refuse a field sobriety test is allowed in most jurisdictions. A refusal to complete a field sobriety test typically does not carry the same serious administrative repercussions as refusing a breathalyzer test, though the officer may then ask for a breath or blood sample.

Will my driver's license be suspended or revoked after a DUI arrest?

There are two types of suspensions. The first is an administrative suspension, which can occur if you refuse a breathalyzer or have a high BAC, meaning you can lose driving privileges even before being found guilty. The second type occurs upon conviction of an intoxicated driving offense, where suspension or revocation depends on your BAC level and whether this is a first offense.

Can I still get auto insurance after a drunk driving conviction?

You will likely find insurance after a first conviction, although your rates will increase significantly. You may also be required to obtain an SR-22 certificate of financial responsibility, which proves your auto insurance policy meets the minimum liability coverage required by state law. A fee is typically charged to file this form.

Can I beat a drunk driving charge in IL?

Yes, it is possible, though it requires a thorough understanding of the law and the technical nature of field sobriety, breath, blood, and urine tests. Identifying technical or human-made errors can highlight the unreliability of results. Additionally, if your constitutional rights were violated, it can lead to the inadmissibility of evidence, potentially resulting in a dismissal or acquittal.

Can I just plead guilty to drunk driving?

You can plead guilty at an arraignment, but it is often a mistake to do so without legal counsel. Pleading guilty immediately means you lose the opportunity to fight the charge, negotiate a plea deal for a lesser offense, or mitigate the sentencing. Subsequent DUI convictions lead to much harsher penalties, so it is critical to protect your record from the start.

Costs vary based on the lawyer's experience, whether the case goes to trial, and the need for expert witnesses. Attorneys may charge by the hour or a flat rate. Please call our office to discuss fee structures and consultation arrangements.

Domestic Violence Defense in IL

What you think is just an argument can turn into something much more. Domestic violence in IL carries serious civil and criminal consequences. If you have been charged in Lake County, it is imperative to contact a domestic violence defense attorney immediately to uphold your rights.

What constitutes domestic violence in IL?

Domestic violence describes harm committed in the context of a domestic relationship (spouses, intimate partners, relatives, or sometimes roommates). It includes physical acts like punching or shoving, but can extend to threats of violence, sexual abuse, emotional abuse, and financial abuse.

What are the immediate consequences of alleged domestic violence?

The court can issue a protection order (restraining or no-contact order) even before you are found guilty. This can restrict your contact with the victim and children, and may require you to leave the family home. Additionally, federal law prohibits anyone subject to a protective order from owning or possessing a firearm.

What are the penalties for a domestic violence conviction?

Penalties depend on the severity of the allegations and prior history, but generally include fines, probation, mandatory treatment or counseling programs, and imprisonment. Courts consider aggravating circumstances like the level of injury, use of a weapon, or if a child witnessed the crime.

Can domestic violence charges be dropped?

Charges can be dropped, but not simply because the alleged victim no longer wants to press charges. Charges are brought by the state, not the affected person. Similarly, a restraining order remains in effect regardless of the victim's current wishes, and violating it is a serious criminal offense.

What are common defenses to domestic violence allegations?

Defenses depend on the case circumstances but commonly include: self-defense or defense of others, lack of sufficient evidence, false allegations, or accidental injury. A conviction can have a long-lasting impact on personal relationships, parenting, and employment, making legal counsel essential.

Spousal Maintenance in IL

The financial aspect of divorce weighs heavily on many minds. Spousal support (alimony) may be established to prevent a financially dependent spouse from succumbing to a lower standard of living. Contact Bur-Men Law Group at (224) 637-8872 to schedule a consultation and discuss your rights.

What is spousal maintenance (alimony) in IL?

Spousal support, maintenance, and alimony describe a court-ordered payment from one spouse to another during or after a divorce. In Lake County, these are referred to as Spousal Maintenance. It can be temporary or permanent, and paid periodically or as a non-modifiable lump sum/property transfer.

What factors determine the amount of spousal maintenance?

Important factors include: the length of the marriage, age and physical/mental condition of each spouse, income disparity, the dependent spouse's ability to secure employment, professional skills, the couple's standard of living, individual assets, and the needs of any children.

When does spousal maintenance terminate?

Maintenance may terminate on an agreed-upon end date, or if: the supported spouse remarries or cohabitates, or either spouse dies. A significant change in circumstances may also prompt a case-by-case termination, which requires supporting evidence.

What are defenses to challenge spousal maintenance?

Reasons to deny or challenge a spousal support claim can include marital misconduct, adultery, bigamy, cruelty (including domestic violence), abandonment, or a felony conviction. If contested, the judge has the final say, making out-of-court agreements highly advisable to avoid trial expenses.

How is spousal maintenance enforced?

Once signed by a judge, the order is enforceable, often through automatic wage withholding. If the paying spouse fails to pay, they can be held in contempt of court, facing fines and penalties. The supported spouse can file a show-cause action to enforce the order.
